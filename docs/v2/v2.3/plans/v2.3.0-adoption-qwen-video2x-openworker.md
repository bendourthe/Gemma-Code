## Overview

This plan turns the comparison's highest-value local capability into a Nexus feature. Video enhancement is added as a post-generation child job, not as a new generation engine. The internal service stays backend-neutral, the first adapter may call a separately installed Video2X executable on supported Windows/Linux hardware, and no Video2X binary or AGPL code is bundled until a distribution review explicitly permits it. The workflow preserves the original output, validates the enhanced media, and tells users that super-resolution and interpolation synthesize detail and frames rather than recover ground truth.

The plan also closes the other two comparison decisions without expanding product scope. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is documented as a rejected v2.3.0 catalog candidate with measurable future admission gates. OpenWorker's new security coworkers are not integrated; instead, a release-scoped handoff specifies the small scanner-coverage and independent-rescan improvements that belong upstream in Nexus-Hub. Nexus-AI consumes those changes later through the existing versioned Hub sync rather than modifying a sibling repository under the wrong commit history.

Success is a packaged local workflow with automated contract, queue, UI, metadata, and benchmark evidence. Human perceptual review and platform-specific manual checks wait until the mandatory final phase, after the implementation is complete.

## Goals First

**Target problem**: Users can generate a local video but cannot improve low-resolution or low-frame-rate output inside Nexus, while experimental model and external-agent announcements create pressure to add capabilities before their licensing, hardware, and security evidence fit the product.

**Target persona**: A privacy-focused developer or creator using Nexus on a consumer Windows, Linux, or macOS computer who wants honest local enhancement without uploading media or losing the original generation.

**Observable definition of done**: A supported machine can enhance a completed Video Lab output locally with upscale, interpolation, or both; progress and cancellation work; the result is separately downloadable and provenance-linked; unsupported cases fail closed with actionable copy; benchmark evidence records quality, duration, and resource use; Qwen3.8 is absent from user-facing catalogs; and the Nexus-Hub security refinement has an upstream-ready handoff with no cross-repository write hidden in this plan.

**Recommended effort level**: max

#### 3.1 - Enhancement child-job runtime (T008)

## Residual Risks

- Video2X licensing may keep the adapter user-installed indefinitely; the backend-neutral contract protects the product from that outcome.
- macOS may remain unsupported until a lawful native backend and measured Vulkan/Metal path are proven.
- Super-resolution and interpolation can create visually plausible but incorrect detail, flicker, ghosting, warping, or damaged text.
- 4K/60 processing can exceed consumer VRAM/RAM or take substantially longer than generation; benchmarks, not marketing copy, define the support envelope.
- The Nexus-Hub security refinement depends on a separate upstream plan and release. Nexus-AI records the handoff but does not claim the upstream work landed without version evidence.
- Qwen artifacts, licenses, and runtimes can change after this plan. Re-evaluation must use fresh official evidence and Nexus benchmarks.

## Section 1: Executive Summary

Six simultaneous releases sketch where the local-AI ecosystem moved in mid-2026, and they cluster into three signals for Nexus.

**Signal 1 -- the 30B-total / ~3-4B-active MoE class is now the local agentic sweet spot.** Muse Glimmer (S2, Apache 2.0, SWE-Bench Verified 76.0, Ollama-supported), Nemotron 3.5 Lightning (S4, OpenMDW-1.1, 1M context, official ggml GGUF, Ollama >= 0.32.9), and DiffusionGemma (S6, Apache 2.0, discrete diffusion, runtime not yet mainlined) are all 26-30B MoE models aimed at consumer GPUs. Two of the three are drop-in catalog candidates through Nexus's existing Ollama channel with zero new infrastructure; the third is a watch item until llama.cpp PR #24423 mainlines. Muse Glimmer in particular would likely displace Qwen 2.5 Coder as the strongest local coding model in the catalog's 24 GB tier.

**Signal 2 -- routing is the new differentiator, and it is pure control logic.** NeMo Switchyard (S4, Apache 2.0) ships three tuning-free routing heuristics (LLM-classifier with session affinity, tool-activity stage routing, cheap-first escalation on sustained difficulty) that map one-to-one onto signals Nexus already collects in `core/telemetry/TelemetryBus.ts`, `core/observability/InferenceMetrics.ts`, and `src/tools/AgentLoop.ts`. The Rust proxy itself duplicates Nexus's v1.16.0 serving gateway and is rejected; the heuristics are the highest-leverage reverse-engineering candidate in this report.

**Signal 3 -- the "local AI studio" category is converging, so defend the moats and close the two real gaps.** Unsloth Desktop (S5) and InvokeAI (S3) show competitors converging on chat + serve + generate; Buzz (S1) shows agents becoming first-class identities. Nexus's durable moats (hardware-aware installer/catalog, quant ladder, per-model harness selector, GPU scheduler, skill self-optimization loop) appear in none of the six sources. The two genuine capability gaps: (a) **local fine-tuning** -- Unsloth Core (Apache 2.0) as an installer-provisioned runtime, like Ollama, under a Nexus-original train-on-your-repos/chats UI gated by the existing golden-task runner; and (b) **image-pillar depth** -- InvokeAI's queue/batch, embedded-metadata recall, and SAM2 segmentation-driven inpainting are all reverse-engineerable onto Nexus's existing SANA/SDXL pipelines without adopting InvokeAI's Python stack.

**Overall recommendation**: adopt two catalog models (Muse Glimmer, Nemotron Lightning) plus their harness profiles as the quick wins; reverse-engineer the Switchyard routing heuristics into the harness selector / planner-critic-worker DAG as the headline v2.1.0 engineering item; plan the Unsloth-powered fine-tuning pillar as the one large addition; harvest InvokeAI's queue, metadata-recall, and SAM2 patterns for the Image Studio; and take Buzz's audit-log / agent-identity ideas as small hardening items. Drop everything multi-user, hosted, or wrapper-shaped (Section 7).

## Section 4: Gap Analysis Summary

### 4a: Strengths to preserve (Nexus-only, appearing in none of the six sources)

- Hardware-aware per-VRAM-tier model catalog + one-shot cross-OS installer (S3 leaves VRAM tuning to manual YAML; S5's app has no tiering; S1 has no local inference at all).
- Per-model harness selector (`modules/coding/orchestration/HarnessSelector.ts`) and the quant ladder with extreme-low-bit + disk-offload tiers.
- GPU/VRAM telemetry + cross-pillar GPU scheduler.
- Local skill self-optimization loop (golden-task runner, bounded-edit optimizer) -- the eval discipline none of the six ship.
- Privacy plumbing: `redactSecrets` pre-index filter, credential vault, local-only telemetry.
- Governance note: InvokeAI's hosted business collapsed into Adobe and Buzz outsources inference to cloud agent runtimes -- both validate the local-first, originality-over-wrappers posture.

### 4b: The two structural gaps

1. **No training capability** (UN1/UN2): every competitor motion in S5 points at fine-tune-on-your-own-data as the next studio pillar; Nexus's eval loop makes its version defensibly better.
2. **Image Studio depth** (IV1-IV5): the chat-first UX is ahead of InvokeAI ergonomically but behind it in queueing, provenance/recall, segmentation-assisted editing, and VRAM-budget knobs.

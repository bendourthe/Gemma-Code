## Overview

This milestone operationalizes the adoption plan from the Liquid AI LFM model library comparison (Section 7) in its recommended sequence (Section 8). The comparison found that Nexus's Agentic tier bottoms out at ~7 GB VRAM (the qwen2.5-coder 7B class entries in `core/registry/catalog.json`), so machines below that get a chat model but no dedicated tool-calling-capable agentic option. LFM2.5-2.6B - a 2.6B open-weight agentic model that plans, calls tools, and runs multi-step tasks in under 2.5 GB of memory (Q4_K_M GGUF is 1.67 GB, CPU-capable) - fills that gap exactly.

Because the source is a model library rather than a codebase, adoption is catalog curation, not program-logic reverse-engineering; per Section 6 every candidate classifies as re-full or re-partial (none is skill-native, none is vendor-intrinsic). Four adoption items land, ordered reverse-engineer-first by P-tier and dependency: **A1 (P0)** the LFM2.5-2.6B catalog entry plus low-tier `recommended.json` placement; **A4 (P0, depends on A1)** the LFM Open License v1.0 use-restriction label, reusing the v1.14 `licenseUrl` surface but presented as a use restriction (free commercial use capped at USD 10M annual revenue), not a download gate (`requiresLicense: false` - the weights are ungated); **A3 (P1, depends on A1)** an LFM-aware harness profile in `modules/coding/orchestration/HarnessSelector.ts`, A/B-validated via `HarnessSelectorAb.ts` golden tasks, including empirical resolution of the conflicting 32K-vs-128K context-length claim; **A2 (P2, depends on A1 + A3)** a benchmark bake-off of LFM2.5-8B-A1B against the incumbent 12-14 GB agentic entries, with a catalog entry only on a win. A P3 watchlist item (VL variants, PII-extract Nano) is recorded as notes only - no build.

Delivery stays entirely within existing mechanisms: the curated catalog (`core/registry/catalog.json` + `core/registry/recommended.json`), the SHA-256-pinned HF weights flow (`scripts/installer/build/pin-hf-weights.py`), the installer's catalog invariants and card copy (`scripts/installer/`), and the per-model harness selection layer (v1.12). Nothing is imported: no hosted Liquid inference (N1), no weight redistribution (N2), no vendor SDK (N3) - see "Out of scope / rejected". Vendor-reported benchmark numbers (ToolSandbox 77.83, BFCLv4 56.88, 220 tok/s) must not appear in `whyRecommended` copy until locally reproduced or independently corroborated (Section 9 general considerations). Success looks like: a sub-4-GB, CPU-capable agentic model is installable on every tier with an accurate, visible license-restriction label; the coding orchestrator selects a correct harness profile for it; and the 8B-A1B question is closed either way (adopted on a win or documented as evaluated-and-declined).

This is deliberately a SMALL, shippable cycle (catalog + license-label + harness-profile work), placed as a standalone v1.19.0 because v1.15 (in flight: installer + studio-chat), v1.16 (planned: serving gateway + OCR), v1.17 (motion identity), and v1.18 (comparison-only) are committed to other themes.

## Out of Scope / Rejected

Carried verbatim in substance from the comparison's Section 9; each rejection is grounded in the MCP Registry Policy (`AGENTS.md`) and must NOT be revisited inside this cycle.

- **N1 - Hosted Liquid inference (LEAP platform, hosted API endpoints, playground).** Any routing of Nexus inference through Liquid-hosted endpoints is generation-as-a-service - an MCP Registry Policy hard no - and violates the local-first, zero-outbound posture. Local open weights are the only acceptable consumption path; even Phase 2/3 validation runs must be local-only.
- **N2 - Bundling or redistributing LFM weights inside the Nexus installer.** Redistribution would make Nexus a licensee-distributor under the LFM Open License v1.0 and inherit its commercial-use cap into the product. Rejected: keep the existing pull-at-install-from-official-repo model with SHA-256 pins (MCP Registry Policy bucket 1 local-only posture; v1.14 installer discipline).
- **N3 - Vendor SDK integration (Liquid edge/LEAP SDKs) for on-device execution.** The local internal equivalent already exists (Ollama / llama.cpp GGUF runtime); a vendor SDK duplicates it and adds supply-chain surface for zero capability gain (MCP Registry Policy bucket 3 preference: the internal equivalent wins).

Also out of scope for v1.19.0 (not-applicable, per Section 4): the small chat/instruct variants (1.2B/350M/230M - the chat ladder already reaches CPU-only hardware via gemma4:e2b), the vision-language and audio variants, and the Liquid Nanos beyond the recorded watchlist note.
